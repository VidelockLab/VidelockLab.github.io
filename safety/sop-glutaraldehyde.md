# Glutaraldehyde

*Videlock Laboratory · lab-specific standard operating procedure*

**This is a laboratory-written SOP, not a UCLA EH&S template.** It is pending review with EH&S (laboratorysafety@ehs.ucla.edu) before being filed in the Chemical Hygiene Plan. The signed copy of record, with the PI approval and training signatures, is in the lab safety manual.

UCLA bands glutaraldehyde under the **Corrosives** SOP, which addresses the corrosivity but not the respiratory sensitization and very low vapour irritant threshold that drive the real-world controls. Read this document together with the [UCLA Corrosives SOP](https://ucla.box.com/s/u4n4t9u2vtwkp2176uxh8acac8wdt6q6) and the lab [Chemical Sensitizers SOP](/safety/sops/sensitizers/). **Use is subject to pre-approval by the PI.**

---

## Materials covered

- **Glutaraldehyde, 25% aqueous solution** (111-30-8) — H314, H317, H318, H332, H334, H335, H400, H411.

---

## Hazard description

Glutaraldehyde is corrosive to skin and eyes, and a potent respiratory and skin sensitizer. Its most important practical property is that **it irritates the eyes and airway at concentrations well below what most people can smell reliably** — the ACGIH ceiling limit is 0.05 ppm. **If you can smell it, the exposure is already too high.**

Sensitization to glutaraldehyde is well documented in histology and endoscopy staff and can end a person's ability to work with it. Contact dermatitis and occupational asthma are both reported.

It is a strong aquatic toxicant (H400/H411); **drain disposal is prohibited.**

---

## Personal protective equipment

| Lab coat | Gloves | Eye protection |
|---|---|---|
| Traditional white lab coat plus a chemical-resistant apron when handling volumes over 100 mL. | Nitrile gloves for brief incidental contact; butyl or laminate (e.g. Silver Shield) gloves for immersion or extended handling. **Nitrile breaks through quickly against 25% glutaraldehyde** — double-glove and change often. | Chemical splash **goggles — not safety glasses** — plus a face shield when pouring. |

---

## Engineering controls, equipment and work practices

| | |
|---|---|
| **Fume hood** | All handling of concentrated glutaraldehyde — opening the stock bottle, diluting, filling and emptying fixation vessels — must occur inside a certified chemical fume hood with the sash at the marked working height. **There is no acceptable open-bench operation for the 25% stock.** |
| **Closed vessels** | Keep fixation containers closed whenever they are outside the hood. Transport in a closed secondary container. Do not leave open trays of glutaraldehyde on the bench or in a cold room — **cold rooms recirculate air and have no exhaust.** |
| **Dilution** | Prepare working dilutions in the hood and label them with the concentration and date. Add glutaraldehyde to buffer, not the reverse. |
| **Substitution** | For routine light-microscopy fixation, paraformaldehyde alone is usually adequate and avoids the sensitization risk entirely. Reserve glutaraldehyde for work that genuinely requires its cross-linking (EM, some ultrastructure). |

---

## Labeling and storage

Store the stock bottle tightly closed in a labelled secondary container in a cool, dark, well-ventilated cabinet, segregated from strong oxidizers, ammonia and amines. Refrigerate if the vendor specifies it. Do not store in a shared cold room without secondary containment.

---

## Housekeeping

**Spills.** Glutaraldehyde has a specific neutralizer: **glycine** (or a commercial glutaraldehyde neutralizing powder). Keep it in the spill kit. For a small spill inside the hood, sprinkle glycine over the spill, allow it to react, then absorb and bag as solid hazardous waste. For any spill **outside a fume hood, or larger than about 100 mL**, leave the area, close the door, notify others, and call **9-1-1** from a campus phone or **(310) 825-1491** from a cell phone. Report exposures to EH&S at (310) 825-9797.

**Decontamination.** Wipe contaminated surfaces with detergent and water unless specified otherwise above. Dispose of contaminated towels as solid hazardous waste.

---

## Waste

Collect used glutaraldehyde and rinses in a labelled aqueous hazardous waste container in the fume hood. **Never drain-dispose, even after glycine neutralization**, because of the aquatic toxicity. Bag contaminated absorbent and gloves as solid hazardous waste.

Refer to the [UCLA Chemical Hygiene Plan](https://ehs.ucla.edu/documents/Laboratory) for general waste accumulation requirements.

---

## First aid and emergencies

| | |
|---|---|
| **Skin contact** | Remove contaminated clothing and flush the area with water for at least 15 minutes. Seek medical attention for any burn, or for any rash that appears hours later. |
| **Eye contact** | Flush at an eyewash for at least 15 minutes, holding the eyelids open, and seek emergency medical attention — glutaraldehyde causes serious eye damage. |
| **Inhalation** | Move to fresh air. Any wheeze, chest tightness or shortness of breath needs urgent medical assessment and must be reported — it may represent sensitization. |
| **Reporting** | Report every exposure to the PI and to UCLA Occupational Health, and notify EH&S at (310) 825-9797. |

**Emergency: 9-1-1** from a campus phone, or **(310) 825-1491** from a cell phone · **UCLA EH&S: (310) 825-9797**
