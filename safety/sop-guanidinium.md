# Guanidinium Salt Lysis Reagents

*Videlock Laboratory · lab-specific standard operating procedure*

**This is a laboratory-written SOP, not a UCLA EH&S template.** It is pending review with EH&S (laboratorysafety@ehs.ucla.edu) before being filed in the Chemical Hygiene Plan. The signed copy of record, with the PI approval and training signatures, is in the lab safety manual.

This SOP covers nucleic-acid lysis and binding buffers built on guanidinium salts. Their GHS classification (mostly acute toxicity category 4) does not trigger any UCLA control band, but **the reaction of guanidinium thiocyanate with bleach produces cyanide gas** — a hazard created entirely by how the waste is handled, and that no banded SOP addresses. **Use is subject to pre-approval by the PI.**

---

## Materials covered

- **QIAzol Lysis Reagent** — guanidine thiocyanate (593-84-0) + phenol (108-95-2). **ALSO** covered by the [UCLA Phenol SOP](https://ucla.box.com/s/5fyhbjv5v9deli8fvqf9i8555nmt3sue) and the [Acutely Toxic Corrosives SOP](https://ucla.box.com/s/tb9uvdhl6ag1od8r8c31cfzryujwbs1h), which take precedence for handling; this document adds the waste rules.
- **LYSIS BUFFER**, guanidine thiocyanate (593-84-0).
- **DNeasy Blood & Tissue Kit** — Buffers AL and AW1 are guanidinium salts.
- **Any TRIzol, RLT, RLT Plus, AL, AW1 or similar buffer**, whether or not it is separately inventoried.

---

## Hazard description

Guanidinium thiocyanate reacts with sodium hypochlorite (bleach) to release **cyanogen chloride and hydrogen cyanide**. Contact with strong acid releases hydrogen cyanide and hydrogen sulfide. Both reactions are fast and happen at room temperature in an ordinary waste container or sink.

This is a realistic laboratory accident, not a theoretical one: the usual sequence is decontaminating a bench or a waste container with bleach while guanidinium waste is present, or pouring lysate into a sink that has just been bleached.

The buffers themselves are irritants and acute toxicity category 4. **QIAzol is far more hazardous than the others** because of its phenol content — phenol is absorbed rapidly through intact skin and causes painless chemical burns.

## Personal protective equipment

| Lab coat | Gloves | Eye protection |
|---|---|---|
| Traditional white lab coat, buttoned, sleeves down. | Nitrile gloves, changed immediately on contact. **For QIAzol, double-glove** — nitrile gives only brief protection against phenol. | ANSI Z87.1-compliant safety glasses; chemical splash goggles when handling QIAzol or pouring any volume over 50 mL. |

---

## Engineering controls, equipment and work practices

| | |
|---|---|
| **The bleach rule** | **Never bring bleach into contact with guanidinium waste**, guanidinium-contaminated glassware, or a sink or bench where guanidinium waste has been poured. If a surface needs both, use 70% ethanol or a quaternary disinfectant instead, or bleach first and rinse thoroughly with water before any guanidinium work begins. |
| **Fume hood** | Open QIAzol and any phenol-containing lysis reagent only in a fume hood. Non-phenolic guanidinium buffers may be used on the open bench with the PPE above. |
| **Waste segregation** | Guanidinium waste gets its own clearly labelled container. Do not combine it with halogenated solvent waste, acidic waste, or any bleach-containing stream. |
| **Labelling** | Label the waste container **"Guanidinium thiocyanate — DO NOT ADD BLEACH OR ACID"** in addition to the standard UCLA hazardous waste tag. Post the same warning at the sink if guanidinium work is done nearby. |

---

## Labeling and storage

Store at room temperature in the original closed container, in a labelled secondary container, segregated from bleach, acids and oxidizers. QIAzol must additionally be stored per the UCLA Phenol SOP.

---

## Housekeeping

**Spills.** Absorb with pads and clean with water and detergent only — **NEVER with a bleach-based cleaner**. For a QIAzol or other phenol-containing spill, follow the UCLA Phenol SOP: the spill kit must contain **PEG-400 or glycerol**, since water is not effective for phenol on skin. Bag all cleanup material as solid hazardous waste. For a spill larger than 100 mL, or any spill of phenol-containing reagent outside a hood, evacuate and call **9-1-1** from a campus phone or **(310) 825-1491** from a cell phone.

**Decontamination.** Wipe contaminated surfaces with detergent and water unless specified otherwise above. Dispose of contaminated towels as solid hazardous waste.

---

## Waste

All guanidinium-containing liquid waste goes into a labelled aqueous hazardous waste container — **never down the drain**. Phenol-containing waste (QIAzol, the phenol phase from an extraction) goes into a **separate** labelled container per the UCLA Phenol SOP. Pipette tips, tubes and towels contaminated with these reagents go into solid hazardous waste.

Refer to the [UCLA Chemical Hygiene Plan](https://ehs.ucla.edu/documents/Laboratory) for general waste accumulation requirements.

## First aid and emergencies

| | |
|---|---|
| **Suspected cyanide gas release** | Leave the area immediately, close the door behind you, and call **9-1-1** from a campus phone or **(310) 825-1491** from a cell phone. Tell responders that **guanidinium thiocyanate may have contacted bleach or acid**. Do not re-enter. |
| **Skin contact — phenol-containing reagent** | Remove contaminated clothing. Wipe the skin with **PEG-400 or glycerol**, then wash with copious water. Seek medical attention **even if there is no pain** — phenol burns are initially painless because phenol anaesthetises the nerve endings. |
| **Skin contact — non-phenolic buffer** | Wash with soap and water for 15 minutes. |
| **Eye contact** | Flush at an eyewash for 15 minutes and seek medical attention. |
| **Inhalation** | Move to fresh air; seek medical attention if symptoms persist. Notify EH&S at (310) 825-9797. |

**Emergency: 9-1-1** from a campus phone, or **(310) 825-1491** from a cell phone · **UCLA EH&S: (310) 825-9797**
